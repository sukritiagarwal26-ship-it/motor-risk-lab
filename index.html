The question

An average can hide the decision.

The book contains enough exposure to estimate claim frequency. Large losses make the average claim size much less stable.

Held-out verdict

Model claim counts by risk. Treat severity as a portfolio estimate until the tail is better understood.

The Poisson model improved claim-count deviance on validation and test. A more complex Gamma severity model lost to a constant mean on validation, so the final expected-loss calculation uses the simpler severity estimate.

The model

Build from frequency × severity.

Two familiar CS1 generalized linear models turn a policy’s characteristics into an expected claim cost.

Claim countE[Ni] = exposurei × exp(Xiβ)

Poisson GLM with a log exposure offset. Driver age, vehicle age, power, fuel, region and log population density enter as predictors.

A Gamma log-link GLM was tested. Validation favoured the training-set mean severity, so μS is used in the final calculation.

Expected lossE[Ci] = E[Ni] × E[Si]

Pure expected claim cost before expenses, profit, inflation or a risk margin.

This multiplication assumes frequency and average claim size can be combined as modelled. It is an approximation, not a commercial premium.

Method and limits

A result that can be checked.

The project uses the authors’ linked frequency and severity files, with no manually assembled claims.

01

Audit the join

Check 413,169 unique policies, positive exposure, 16,181 positive claim amounts, every claim linked, and claim counts equal the claim records.

02

Split before fitting

Assign policies 70% train, 15% validation, 15% test with a fixed seed. Claim rows inherit the parent policy split.

03

Fit and select

Fit Poisson frequency and Gamma severity GLMs on training data. Choose components by validation deviance against constant baselines.

04

Test once

Report held-out deviance, aggregate O/E, risk-decile calibration, overdispersion and a policy bootstrap interval.

Read the limits before the result

  • The original insurer is unidentified and the period of this dataset is not specified in its documentation.
  • Some claim amounts are fixed under the French IRSA-IDA settlement convention.
  • The source does not name a currency for claim amounts.
  • No time stamps or event identifiers support catastrophe clustering or time-based validation.
  • The severity tail and any high attachment layer are especially unstable.
